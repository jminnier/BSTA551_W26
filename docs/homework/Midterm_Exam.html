Part I: Conceptual Questions (45 points)

Problem 1: Estimator Properties (15 points)

A pharmaceutical company is developing a new assay to measure drug concentration in blood plasma. Let \(X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n\) be independent measurements from a distribution with mean \(\mu\) (the true concentration) and variance \(\sigma^2\).

Three estimators for \(\mu\) are proposed:

  • \(\hat{\mu}_1 = \bar{X}\) (sample mean)
  • \(\hat{\mu}_2 = \frac{X_1 + X_n}{2}\) (average of first and last observations)
  • \(\hat{\mu}_3 = \frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i\) (weighted sample mean)

(a) (3 points) For each estimator, determine whether it is unbiased for \(\mu\). If biased, calculate the bias.

(b) (4 points) Calculate the variance of each estimator in terms of \(\sigma^2\) and \(n\). Assume the observations are independent.

(c) (4 points) Calculate the Mean Squared Error (MSE) of each estimator. Which estimator has the smallest MSE for \(n = 10\)?

(d) (4 points) As \(n \to \infty\), which estimator(s) are consistent for \(\mu\)? Justify your answer using the definition of consistency.

Problem 2: Fisher Information and the Cramér-Rao Lower Bound (15 points)

A biostatistician models the total time (in weeks) for a patient to complete a two-phase post-operative rehabilitation protocol after hip replacement surgery. Each phase has an independent, identically distributed duration, so the total rehabilitation time follows a Gamma distribution with known shape \(\alpha = 2\) and unknown rate parameter \(\beta > 0\). The pdf is:

\[f(x; \beta) = \beta^2 x \, e^{-\beta x}, \quad x > 0\]

For this distribution, \(E(X) = 2/\beta\) and \(\text{Var}(X) = 2/\beta^2\).

(a) (4 points) Calculate the Fisher Information \(I(\beta)\) for a single observation. Show your derivation using \(I(\beta) = E\left[-\frac{d^2}{d\beta^2}\ell(\beta)\right]\).

(b) (3 points) State the Cramér-Rao Lower Bound (CRLB) for unbiased estimators of \(\beta\) based on \(n\) independent observations.

(c) (4 points) The MLE of \(\beta\) can be shown to be \(\hat{\beta}_{MLE} = 2/\bar{X}\). It can also be shown that \(\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \sim \text{Gamma}(2n, \beta)\), and for \(Y \sim \text{Gamma}(a, b)\), we have \(E(1/Y) = b/(a-1)\) when \(a > 1\).

  • Use these facts to show that \(\hat{\beta}_{MLE}\) is biased, and find the bias.
  • Propose a modified estimator \(\tilde{\beta}\) that IS unbiased for \(\beta\).

(d) (4 points) It can be shown that for \(Y \sim \text{Gamma}(a, b)\), \(\text{Var}(1/Y) = \frac{b^2}{(a-1)^2(a-2)}\) when \(a > 2\). Use this to find \(\text{Var}(\tilde{\beta})\) and determine whether the unbiased estimator achieves the CRLB. What does this result tell us?

Problem 3: Confidence Interval Interpretation (15 points)

A clinical trial investigates the effectiveness of a new statin drug in lowering LDL cholesterol. In a sample of \(n = 64\) patients, the mean reduction in LDL cholesterol is \(\bar{x} = 28\) mg/dL with sample standard deviation \(s = 16\) mg/dL.

(a) (4 points) Construct a 95% confidence interval for the true mean reduction \(\mu\). Justify your choice of interval (z vs. t) and state any assumptions.

(b) (4 points) For each statement below, indicate whether it is a CORRECT or INCORRECT interpretation of your confidence interval. Briefly explain why.

  1. “There is a 95% probability that \(\mu\) lies in this interval.”
  2. “If we repeated this study many times, 95% of the resulting confidence intervals would contain \(\mu\).”
  3. “95% of patients in this study had LDL reductions within this interval.”
  4. “We are 95% confident that the true mean LDL reduction is in this interval.”

(c) (4 points) The drug company wants to claim that the mean LDL reduction is at least 25 mg/dL. Based on your confidence interval, can they make this claim at the 95% confidence level? Explain.

(d) (3 points) If the company wanted a 95% CI with margin of error no greater than 3 mg/dL, what sample size would be required? (Use \(s = 16\) as your estimate of \(\sigma\).)
